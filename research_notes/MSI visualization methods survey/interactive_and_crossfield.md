# Interactive, Web-Based, Showcase and Cross-Field Visualization Ideas for MSI (for SMILE-MSI)

Scope note: research run 2026-10-07. "RECENT" = 2024–2026. Implementation difficulty is rated from a PySide6/pyqtgraph/matplotlib desktop app: **Easy** (days, pure-Python, existing deps), **Medium** (1–3 weeks, new optional dependency), **Hard** (new runtime/JS stack, ML training, or a hosted service).

Baseline of what SMILE-MSI already has (from a grep of `/home/user/SMILE-MSI/smile_msi`, not from the web): a UMAP/t-SNE pixel-embedding "studio" whose tissue map and linked scatter share one **2-D colour wheel** (hue = direction, saturation = distance from the embedding centre; `smile_msi/multivariate.py::_coords_rgb`); freehand **lasso** selection in the embedding scatter (`gui/scatter.py`); freehand-brush and shape ROIs on ion images; RGB overlay; linked montage panels (`gui/montage.py`); 3D serial-section stack (`gui/stack3d.py`); optical overlay/registration; a figure editor. It uses an **internal Zarr v2 ZipStore cube cache** (`cubestore.py`), which is a cache file, not OME-Zarr or SpatialData. A grep found **no** GIF/MP4/animation export, **no** HTML/web export, **no** OME-Zarr/SpatialData export, **no** napari/Vitessce bridge, and **no** sonification. Those absences are where most of the NEW ideas below sit.

## Q1. Interactive viewers and shareable web exports (Vitessce, Viv, napari, TissUUmaps, SpatialData, Minerva, OpenSeadragon, MSI-specific viewers; exporting MSI to OME-Zarr/SpatialData)

### Takeaway
The web-sharing stack in spatial omics has settled on **OME-Zarr/SpatialData on disk, served as static files, rendered client-side by Viv/Vitessce (deck.gl/WebGL) or OpenSeadragon (Minerva, TissUUmaps)**. MSI is only now plugging into it. The most direct route for SMILE-MSI is the **2026 Thyra converter (imzML to SpatialData Zarr, MIT licence)** or a home-grown writer that exports reduced products (ion-image stacks, segmentation labels, ROI shapes, a pixel × feature table) rather than full spectra. Then add a one-click "export Vitessce config" or "open in napari" bridge. None of this exists in SMILE-MSI today, so it is all **NEW**.

### Cited Findings
**Web viewers and frameworks (general spatial omics)**
- **Vitessce** (Keller et al., *Nature Methods* 2024; 2025 issue). DOI 10.1038/s41592-024-02436-x. An integrative web framework of coordinated views. It reads AnnData, MuData, SpatialData, CSV, OME-TIFF and OME-Zarr straight from static web servers or cloud object storage. Its showcase includes a **volumetric rendering of a 3D multimodal imaging-MS dataset alongside a heatmap and side-by-side protein- vs lipid/metabolite-based dimensionality-reduction scatterplots**, so it already demonstrates IMS in linked views. It uses Viv, HiGlass and Vega-Lite underneath. — [Nature Methods](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02436-x); [ResearchGate PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384407644_Vitessce_integrative_visualization_of_multimodal_and_spatially_resolved_single-cell_data)
- **Viv** (Manz et al., *Nature Methods* 2022, "Viv: multiscale visualization of high-resolution multiplexed bioimaging data on the web"; DOI 10.1038/s41592-022-01482-7. The search snippet showed a garbled prefix, and the DOI shown here is the standard Nature Methods form). A JavaScript library that renders OME-TIFF and OME-NGFF (Zarr) directly in the browser through WebGL multichannel rendering, packaged as **deck.gl layers**, with no server-side rendering. Companions: **Avivator** is a pure client-side viewer for OME-TIFF/Zarr over HTTP or local disk. **Vizarr** is a Zarr viewer with a Python backend (imjoy-rpc) that embeds in Jupyter/Colab. npm package: `@hms-dbmi/viv`. — [GitHub hms-dbmi/viv](https://www.github.com/hms-dbmi/viv); [npm](https://npmjs.com/package/%40hms-dbmi%2Fviv)
- **Vitessce Link** (preprint, arXiv 2511.04262, Nov 2025, RECENT). A stereoscopic mixed-reality 3D view synchronised over WebSockets with a 2D Vitessce screen that holds the channel, threshold and filter controls. It runs entirely in the browser. Case studies are nephrology and oncology. The excerpts found do not mention MS data. The same paper notes that Viv/Vizarr do 3D multichannel rendering but lack interactivity between images, segmentations and derived data. — [arXiv 2511.04262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04262v1)
- **Minerva / Minerva Story** (Hoffer, Rashid et al., *JOSS* 2020, DOI 10.21105/joss.02579). A "narrative" image browser for multiplexed tissue. An author builds **waypoints**, each with a text description, position, zoom, shape overlays and a 4–8-channel subset. It adds interactive single-cell scatterplots, bar charts and heatmaps with **two-way linked navigation** to the image. It renders through **OpenSeadragon** (Google-Maps-style pyramids) and deploys to **static web hosting**. — [PMC7989801](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7989801/); [JOSS PDF](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.02579.pdf)
- Rashid et al., *Nature Biomedical Engineering* 2021. Argues for "narrative online guides" for interpreting digital-pathology and tissue-atlas images, demonstrated with Minerva. — [Springer link](https://link.springer.com/10.1038/s41551-021-00789-8); [The Pathologist summary](https://thepathologist.com/diagnostics/changing-the-narrative-of-multiplex-tissue-imaging)
- **TissUUmaps 3** (*Heliyon* 2023). A free, browser-based, GPU-accelerated viewer for 10^7+ points over tissue images. It can be customised, shared and **embedded in Jupyter notebooks**, and runs as a web service or locally. It has plugins and modules for markers, regions, spatial statistics and in-situ-transcriptomics QC. It is on PyPI (`TissUUmaps`, e.g., 3.2.1.x). — [PMC10149187](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10149187/); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/TissUUmaps/3.2.1.3)
- **SpatialData storage model**: five SpatialElements (images, labels, points, shapes, tables), all stored as OME-Zarr. — [Vitessce/SpatialData coverage via search, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/384407644_Vitessce_integrative_visualization_of_multimodal_and_spatially_resolved_single-cell_data) (the SpatialData paper itself, Marconato et al., *Nature Methods* 2024/25, was not fetched in this session.)

**MSI into OME-Zarr / SpatialData**
- **Thyra** (M4i Maastricht; bioRxiv **Jan 2026**, RECENT; DOI 10.64898/2026.01.26.700792). A Python library that converts MSI (imzML/ibd, Bruker timsTOF/solariX/rapifleX `.d`, Waters `.raw`, PHI nanoTOF ToF-SIMS, experimental mzPeak) into a SpatialData `.zarr`. Contents: every pixel's spectrum on **one shared m/z axis** as a table (`msi_dataset_z0`, rows = pixels, columns = m/z bins), a TIC image, the optical slide image when present, ion mobility and MS/MS for timsTOF, and a validated acquisition description for METASPACE. API: `from thyra import convert_msi; convert_msi("x.imzML", "x.zarr")`, then `spatialdata.read_zarr`. **MIT licence**; `pip install thyra` (Python 3.12–3.14). The preprint says outputs feed Squidpy, Scanpy, napari, Vitessce (via "easy-vitessce") and Harpy. The README does not mention napari or Vitessce. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/M4i-Imaging-Mass-Spectrometry/thyra); [bioRxiv PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.26.700792v1.full.pdf)
- A 2025/26 review of MS-based human spatial omics reports that **OME-Zarr usage for MSI is scarce**. imzML can be converted to OME-Zarr by Cytomine's PIMS plugin **"pims-plugin-format-msi"**, and future OME-Zarr specs are expected to add an **explicit m/z axis**. **HuBMAP uses OME-TIFF + Vitessce** to show MSI with other modalities. METASPACE, KPMP and HuBMAP form a federated MSI ecosystem. — [PMC12879364](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12879364/)
- HuBMAP/KPMP kidney work uses MALDI IMS with autofluorescence-microscopy segmentation of functional tissue units (webinar deck, 2022). — [dkNET webinar slides](https://www.slideshare.net/dkNET/dknet-webinar-the-human-biomolecular-atlas-program-hubmap-10142022)

**MSI-specific viewers/tools**
- **MSI-Explorer** (*Analytical Chemistry* 2025, RECENT; DOI 10.1021/acs.analchem.5c01513). The first napari plugin for MSI. It covers imzML import, metadata, visualisation, mean intensity, ROI analysis, database annotation and preprocessing. The authors note napari had no native MSI support. — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.analchem.5c01513); [PubMed 40476501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40476501/); [napari-hub](https://napari-hub.org/plugins/msi-explorer.html)
- **MSiReader v1.0** (*JASMS* 2018, DOI 10.1007/s13361-017-1809-6). MATLAB (plus a standalone build), vendor-neutral. Adds a quantification tool, **image overlay with other modalities**, and a **mass-accuracy heatmap**. No "v2" was found. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s13361-017-1809-6)
- **Cardinal** (Bemis et al., *Bioinformatics* 2015). R/Bioconductor statistical MSI toolbox (segmentation, classification) with visualisation. — [PMC4495298](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4495298/)
- **METASPACE** (EMBL). A web annotation platform giving FDR-controlled annotations, MSM scores, ion images and isotope images side by side. It holds more than 10,000 public datasets. **METASPACE-ML** rescoring is newer. There is a Python API (`metaspace2020`) for fetching isotopic images. — [PMC8401310](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8401310/); [metaspace2020 docs](https://metaspace2020.readthedocs.io/en/latest/content/examples/fetch-isotopic-images.html); [Thermo ASMS'23 poster](https://assets.thermofisher.com/TFS-Assets/CMD/Flyers/fl-489242-asms23-spectra-molecules-spatial-metabolomics-annotationap-smaldi-orbitrap-data-metaspace-fl489242-en.pdf)
- **Waters "MSI Analyte Browser"** is a web micro-app for 2D MSI visualisation. Older Computis-project tools include SpectViewer, Data Cube Explorer and EasyMSI. **MassVision** (bioRxiv 2025) is an open-source end-to-end AI MSI platform. — [Waters microapp](https://microapps.on-demand.waters.com/home/showmarkdown/msi-analyte-browser); [Computis tools (HAL)](https://hal-cea.archives-ouvertes.fr/cea-03086461v1); [MassVision bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.01.29.635489.full.pdf)
- **"SpectralBrowser"**: no tool by that name was found. The closest are SpectralAnalysis (Race et al.) and the tools listed above. — [MSiReader context via bioinformaticshome](https://bioinformaticshome.com/tools/proteomics/descriptions/MSiReader.html)

### Inferences
Catalog entries are rated by difficulty and by whether they are NEW for SMILE-MSI.
- **SpatialData/OME-Zarr export of reduced products** (ion-image stack for N chosen lipids as a multiscale OME-Zarr image; segmentation as `labels`; ROIs as `shapes`; a pixel × lipid AnnData `table` with UMAP coords in `obsm` and cluster IDs in `obs`). **NEW. Medium.** Optional deps: `spatialdata`, `ome-zarr`, `anndata`. Thyra could be used directly for raw-cube export (**Easy–Medium**, but it requires Python ≥3.12). Exporting reduced products, not the full spectral cube, keeps files small and is what Vitessce/napari render well.
- **"Share as web page"**: write a Vitessce JSON config next to the Zarr store and point users to vitessce.io or a static host. **NEW. Medium.** It needs no JS code in the app, because Vitessce configs are JSON (the `vitessce` Python package can generate them; not verified this session). It gives image + UMAP scatter + heatmap linked views in a browser.
- **Minerva-style narrative "story" export** (waypoints = saved view state + caption + channel set). **NEW. Medium–Hard.** A lightweight variant is easy: export a self-contained HTML with OpenSeadragon (single JS file from cdnjs) and pre-rendered PNG tiles per waypoint.
- **"Open in napari" button** that passes the ion-image stack, labels and ROIs. **NEW. Easy–Medium.** napari is Qt-based, so it can share the process with PySide6, though Qt version pinning is a risk. MSI-Explorer shows there is demand.
- **TissUUmaps export** (points = pixels coloured by cluster over the optical image). **NEW. Medium.** It overlaps with the Vitessce option. Pick one.
- **Static self-contained HTML with Plotly/Bokeh** (ion image + spectrum + UMAP with linked selection, embedded data). **NEW. Easy–Medium.** This is the lowest-friction shareable-interactive option for small datasets, with no server and no Zarr.

## Q2. Linked brushing, lens/magnifier interactions, and animated storytelling

### Takeaway
Two-way linked brushing between embedding plots and the tissue image is now standard in Minerva, Vitessce and cytomapper's Shiny gating app. SMILE-MSI **already has** the core (a shared colour wheel between UMAP and the tissue map, plus lasso). What is **missing** is (a) a live **spectrum panel** that updates from a brushed selection and highlights in the image, (b) a **lens/magnifier** that shows a different channel or modality inside a movable circle, and (c) **animated GIF/MP4 export** (m/z sweep, 3D turntable, story fly-through). No dedicated "spectral lens" MSI tool was found in the literature.

### Cited Findings
- Minerva authors add interactive single-cell scatterplots, bar charts, heatmaps and cell outlines with **two-way linked navigation** between plots and points in the image. — [PMC7989801](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7989801/)
- Vitessce's core design is coordinated multiple views. Its IMS showcase links a 3D volume, a heatmap and DR scatterplots. — [Nature Methods 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02436-x)
- **cytomapper** (IMC, R/Bioconductor) includes a **Shiny app for hierarchical gating**: select cells by marker expression, and they are shown in the corresponding images. It is part of the Bodenmiller end-to-end workflow (Windhager et al., *Nature Protocols* 2023) with steinbock, napari-imc and imcRtools. histoCAT is an alternative interactive viewer. — [PubMed 37816904](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37816904/); [Bioconductor cytomapper](https://code.bioconductor.org/browse/cytomapper/RELEASE_3_11)
- Vitessce Link synchronises a 3D mixed-reality view with 2D controls across devices, a form of cross-device linking (RECENT, 2025 preprint). — [arXiv 2511.04262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.04262v1)
- In the Fonville hyperspectral-visualisation lineage, embedding-to-colour maps make "pixels with similar molecular profiles … displayed with similar colors". This is the principle behind SMILE-MSI's shared colour wheel. — [ResearchGate: Fonville et al., Anal Chem 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233948006_Hyperspectral_Visualization_of_Mass_Spectrometry_Imaging_Data)

### Inferences
- **Brush to live mean spectrum, plus a difference spectrum versus the rest of the tissue.** Lasso in UMAP or on the image, then plot that selection's mean spectrum against the complement with the top differential peaks labelled. Clicking a peak sets the ion image. **Partly NEW** (SMILE-MSI has ROI spectra and ROI statistics, but probably not as a live brush-driven panel). **Easy** in pyqtgraph using the existing lasso signals.
- **Spectral/modal lens.** A circular movable ROI (pyqtgraph `CircleROI`) inside which the image swaps to another channel (H&E/optical vs ion image, a second lipid, the UMAP colour map, or a virtual stain). A "magnifier lens" variant shows an upsampled crop. **NEW. Easy.** No published MSI precedent was found, so this would be distinctive.
- **Hover spectrum tooltip** (pixel spectrum sparkline under the cursor). **NEW or partial. Easy.**
- **Animated exports.** (1) An m/z "scan" GIF that sweeps an ion image across a lipid class or a mass-defect series. (2) A 3D serial-section **turntable** MP4 from `stack3d`. (3) A cross-fade between optical and ion image. (4) A cluster-reveal animation. **NEW. Easy–Medium.** Use `imageio`/`imageio-ffmpeg` and render frames with matplotlib or `QWidget.grab()`. MP4 needs an ffmpeg binary (imageio-ffmpeg bundles one).
- **Story mode in-app** (Minerva waypoints as an in-app slideshow with captions, exportable as MP4 or HTML). **NEW. Medium.**

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed MSI paper presenting a "spectral lens" or magnifier interaction was found. Treat this as an open niche, not established practice.
- No source was found on publication norms for animated GIF/MP4 MSI figures (e.g., journal supplementary-video conventions).

## Q3. Sonification and unconventional encodings of spectra

### Takeaway
Mass-spec sonification exists but is niche: one 2025 RCM feasibility paper on FT-ICR and a few hobby/open-source tools. **No MSI-specific (spatial) sonification was found.** Astronomy has a more mature "listen to a spectral datacube" literature that maps directly onto MSI pixel spectra. Sonification would be **NEW** and a memorable outreach or demo feature, though of low analytical value.

### Cited Findings
- **Arpino et al., *Rapid Communications in Mass Spectrometry*** (≈2025, DOI 10.1002/rcm.10047). "Sonified Signals From a Compact FT-ICR Instrument: A Feasibility Study. I—Data Mapping to an Equal-Tempered Chromatic Scale". The authors report knowing of no earlier attempts at audible MS data. They down-shift instrument frequencies (~100–1000 kHz) into 20 Hz–20 kHz and propose **"molechords"** so that ions mapping to the same note stay distinguishable. — [Wiley](https://analyticalsciencejournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rcm.10047?af=R)
- **ms_music** (GitHub, Python). Converts mzML to audio using a gradient method (continuous sine synthesis) or ADSR event synthesis, with musical quantisation and MIDI export. — [GitHub mmattano/ms_music](https://github.com/mmattano/ms_music)
- Astronomy: **"Inspecting spectra with sound: proof-of-concept & extension to datacubes"** (Trayford et al., *RAS Techniques & Instruments* 2023). Builds sounds as superposed sines per spectrum and extends this to datacubes. A survey of 58 respondents found that listener ratings correlate with S/N, line width and flux ratios. — [OUP RASTI](https://academic.oup.com/rasti/article/2/1/387/7209921); [arXiv 2306.10126](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.10126)
- "Interactive Multimodal Integral Field Spectroscopy" (arXiv 2412.00185, 2024) does interactive audio-visual exploration of IFS datacubes, the astronomy analogue of an MSI cube. — [arXiv 2412.00185](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.00185)
- Phonon-spectra sonification (arXiv 2603.29037, 2026) reports a trade-off: direct spectral mapping was most accurate but least pleasant, and musical mappings were pleasant but less informative. — [arXiv 2603.29037](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29037v1)

### Inferences
- **"Listen to a pixel / an ROI"**: map the m/z axis to pitch (log-scaled) and intensity to amplitude, then play while hovering. Alternatively scan the cursor across the tissue and render a WAV, giving an "audible line-scan" like the IFS datacube work. **NEW. Easy** (numpy sine synthesis to WAV via the stdlib `wave` module; QtMultimedia for playback). Value is outreach and accessibility, not quantitation.
- Lipid-class chords (PC/PE/PI as different timbres) adapt Arpino's "molechord" idea. **NEW. Medium.**

### Gaps
- No MSI (spatially resolved) sonification paper was found. No user studies exist for MS sonification beyond the astronomy analogues.

## Q4. Cross-pollination from IMC/MIBI/CODEX/spatial transcriptomics and from astronomy/remote-sensing hyperspectral imaging

### Takeaway
MSI already borrowed the key remote-sensing idea: **hyperspectral "embedding-to-colour" visualisation** (Fonville 2013, then spatially mapped t-SNE in 2016, then UMAP/SOM variants), which SMILE-MSI implements as its colour wheel. A 2025 preprint pushes back with "truthful" alternatives (TOP3). The richest untapped sources are (a) **astronomy colour-composite craft**: chromatic ordering, per-band arcsinh stretch, "representative colour", and building colour per group of bands before combining; (b) **multiplexed-imaging composite conventions**: additive pseudocolour of 4–8 channels with per-channel windowing, as in Minerva, IMC and CODEX; and (c) **spatial-transcriptomics-style dot/segment rendering** of pixels over high-resolution H&E. The "Hubble palette" (SHO mapping) has not been formally applied to MSI.

### Cited Findings
**Hyperspectral and embedding colour in MSI**
- **Fonville et al., *Analytical Chemistry* 2013, 85(3)**, "Hyperspectral Visualization of Mass Spectrometry Imaging Data". Brings remote-sensing colour-coding into MSI so that spectrally similar pixels get similar colours. Applied to PCA, SOM and t-SNE. Citing works describe colouring each pixel by its t-SNE position in perceptually linear **L\*a\*b\*** space, or reading a 3-D embedding as RGB. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233948006_Hyperspectral_Visualization_of_Mass_Spectrometry_Imaging_Data)
- **Abdelmoula et al., *PNAS* 2016**. Spatially mapped t-SNE identified prognostic tumour subpopulations. It credits Fonville with introducing t-SNE to MSI. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1510227113. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1510227113)
- **Colour-tagged toroidal SOMs** for ToF-SIMS reduce the data to one RGB image and are benchmarked against t-SNE and UMAP. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336026213_Visualizing_ToF-SIMS_Hyperspectral_Imaging_Data_Using_Color-Tagged_Toroidal_Self-Organizing_Maps)
- An IEEE 2021 (OSTI) comparison of t-SNE vs UMAP for colourising hyperspectral images found that **UMAP gave a clear contrast advantage**. Both methods are sensitive to object size and scene content, which limits generalisability. — [OSTI 1899480](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1899480)
- **"Truthful visualizations for mass spectrometry imaging"** (bioRxiv 2025, 10.1101/2025.03.18.643852, v4, RECENT). Proposes **TOP3**: take the three largest intensities per pixel and convert LAB to RGB, as a counterpoint to embedding-based colouring. The full text could not be fetched (bioRxiv egress blocked); the details come from the search snippet. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.18.643852v4.full.pdf)

**Astronomy colour-composite craft**
- **Rector, Levay, Frattare et al., "Image-Processing Techniques for the Creation of Presentation-Quality Astronomical Images"** (*Astronomical Journal* 2007; arXiv astro-ph/0412138). Defines **"natural/true" vs "representative/mapped" colour** and recommends trying many versions. KPNO alone has more than 100 filters. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0412138)
- **Rector, Levay, Frattare, Arcand, Watzke, "The Aesthetics of Astrophysics: How to Make Appealing Color-Composite Images that Convey the Science"** (*PASP* 2017; arXiv 1703.00490). For multiwavelength data, **build a chromatically ordered full-colour image per band group and then combine them**, instead of putting radio, optical and X-ray directly into R, G and B. Direct mapping makes stars an unnatural green. Overlaying models (e.g., lensing dark matter in blue) can mislead viewers. Colour choice carries meaning (blue = hot, red = cool; violet for "ethereal"). — [arXiv 1703.00490](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.00490); [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1703.00490)
- **Lupton et al., *PASP* 2004** (DOI 10.1086/382245). An **arcsinh stretch** shows faint objects while preserving the structure of bright ones, the standard way to build high-dynamic-range RGB composites. — [doi.org/10.1086/382245](https://www.doi.org/10.1086/382245)
- An ESO-based paper (CAP journal) proposes six aesthetic criteria for astro colour images: photogenic resolution, definition/contrast, colour, composition, S/N, and artefact removal. — [CAP journal](https://www.capjournal.org/issues/14/14_20.pdf)
- "Hubble palette" example: Levay's Pillars of Creation maps O→blue, H→green, S→red (secondary photography-site account, lower reliability). — [PictureCorrect](https://picturecorrect.com/?p=50333)

**Multiplexed imaging (IMC/MIBI/CODEX)**
- The Minerva convention is to layer 4–8 channels per view, rendered as an additive pseudocolour composite over OpenSeadragon pyramids. — [PMC7989801](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7989801/)
- The IMC toolchain (steinbock → cytomapper/napari-imc/histoCAT → imcRtools) renders multichannel pixel images and **cell-level values painted onto segmentation masks**. — [PubMed 37816904](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37816904/); [Univ. Bern IMC platform](https://www.imc.unibe.ch/technologies/hyperion/data_analysis)
- Hyperspectral optical microscopy is getting napari tools for spectral rendering and unmixing (CZI-funded "Rainbow"). — [CZI](https://chanzuckerberg.com/science/programs-resources/imaging/napari/spectral-image-visualization-and-analysis/)

### Inferences
- **N-channel additive composite (more than 3 ions), Minerva/IMC-style.** Each lipid gets an arbitrary hue, its own window/gamma, and additive blending on black, with a channel legend. SMILE-MSI's RGB overlay is limited to 3 channels. **NEW (extension). Easy.**
- **Astronomy-style "representative colour" composite with arcsinh stretch.** Per-ion arcsinh/asinh-Lupton stretch, then a **chromatically ordered hue assignment** (low m/z → blue … high m/z → red, or by lipid class), then additive combine. `astropy.visualization.make_lupton_rgb` already implements the Lupton recipe and can be used for 3 channels. **NEW. Easy.** This gives the "nebula" look while staying principled.
- **"Hubble/SHO palette" preset**: map three lipid classes (e.g., PC, PE, SM sums) into a fixed narrowband-style palette (gold/teal/blue). **NEW. Easy**, but cosmetic. Label it as representative colour per Rector et al.
- **TOP3 truthful colouring** as an alternative to UMAP colouring, and as a sanity check against embedding artefacts. **NEW. Easy.** It is quick to implement but should be checked against the full preprint text.
- **L\*a\*b\* embedding colouring** (Fonville lineage) as an option beside SMILE-MSI's HSV colour wheel. **Partly NEW. Easy.** Perceptual uniformity supports a better "similar colour = similar spectrum" claim.

## Q5. Generative/aesthetic approaches: MSI art, journal cover art, virtual staining (MSI to histology and histology to MSI)

### Takeaway
The headline RECENT result is **Zhang, Huang … Ozcan, *Science Advances* 2025, "Virtual staining of label-free tissue in imaging mass spectrometry"**. A diffusion model generates histology-like stain images from MALDI IMS of unstained human kidney. Note that the target stain is **PAS**, not H&E. The reverse direction (H&E → MSI images) is much less developed: one prostate-cancer multimodal deep-learning study was found. Verified journal-cover evidence for MSI was thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Virtual staining of label-free tissue in IMS**: *Science Advances* 11(31): eadv0741, 1 Aug 2025; DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adv0741; arXiv 2411.13120 (Nov 2024). UCLA/Vanderbilt/TU Delft (Ozcan group). A **diffusion model** adds cellular morphology to IMS of unstained tissue. In blind tests on human kidney, virtual stains matched **PAS** histochemistry for key renal structures **despite ~10× larger IMS pixel size**. Optimised noise sampling at inference improves repeatability. The tissue stays intact for further analysis. — [PMC (pmid 40749063)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/40749063); [arXiv 2411.13120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13120v1); [UCLA ECE](https://www.ee.ucla.edu/deep-learning-advances-imaging-mass-spectrometry-with-virtual-histological-detail/); [phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2025-08-deep-advances-imaging-mass-spectrometry.html)
- **H&E + MSI deep learning (prostate)** (Haque et al., OSTI/bioRxiv 2022). Registers WSI H&E features to MSI (FT-ICR, 120 µm). Predicts cancer from H&E only, MSI only, or both. Describes a "physically constrained" cross-modal model that can reconstruct high-resolution images and predict spectra. — [OSTI 1885240](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1885240); [bioRxiv 2022.05.11.491570](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.05.11.491570.full.pdf)
- A 2025 brain-lipid MSI preprint notes that H&E is "fully blind to lipids", which limits H&E → lipid-image prediction. — [bioRxiv 2025.09.12.675752](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.09.12.675752v2.full)

### Inferences
- **Virtual staining in SMILE-MSI**: training a diffusion model is **Hard** and out of scope. A feasible **NEW, Medium** feature is a **"pseudo-H&E" rendering** that maps two chosen lipid or segment channels through the standard Beer-Lambert H&E colour model (hematoxylin-blue/eosin-pink optical-density composition, as used in false-H&E for fluorescence microscopy). This is a deterministic "molecular histology look", clearly labelled as not a stain prediction. (The false-H&E technique for microscopy is known from prior knowledge and was not sourced this session.)
- **Cover-art preset**: high-DPI, borderless, black-background additive composite with arcsinh stretch, optional glow (Gaussian bloom), and a vignette, exported at journal cover aspect ratios (e.g., ACS cover ~ 8.5×11 in). **NEW. Easy.**
